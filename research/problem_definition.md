# Problem Definition — Groww SIP Retention

## Thinking evolution

Business Metric → Product Outcomes → AI Discovery → Primary Research → Problem Definition

1. **Business metric:** % of first-time MF investors with active SIP at 12 months.
2. **Product outcomes:** mandate reliability, monthly debit success, no active pause/stop, no passive lapse, expectation durability, progress visibility, re-activation.
3. **AI Discovery (1,091 public items):** pause/stop intent is the most prevalent theme (57%); users want to pause but app only offers stop; irregular income breaks fixed monthly debits; expectations set by influencers mismatch first-year reality.
4. **Primary research (6 interviews):** confirmed pause-vs-stop gap; added silent lapse from insufficient funds; daily-check anxiety driven by push notifications; "set and forget" without review guidance.
5. **Problem definition (below).**

## Target user segment
First-time mutual fund investors, age 22–30, early-career or irregular income (salaried juniors, freelancers, gig workers), SIP ≤ ₹1,000/month, influenced by friends/YouTube, in months 3–8 of their first SIP.

## The moment we are solving for
**Months 3–6 of the first SIP**, when the investor receives their first flat or negative short-term statement AND/OR hits a tight month where the fixed SIP debit is hard to cover. This is when good intent meets the first reality check, and the investor decides whether to continue, pause, stop, or silently lapse.

## Product outcome we intend to influence
**O3: No active pause / stop** — reduce the share of first-time SIP investors who actively pause or stop their SIP in months 3–12, by giving them a graceful middle option and re-framing the moment of doubt.

## Root cause
The product forces a binary choice at the moment of doubt: **keep paying every month** or **stop forever**. There is no "pause this month," no "reduce temporarily," and no explanation that short-term flat/negative returns are normal in year 1. For irregular-income users, a failed debit silently ends the SIP with no nudge to resume. The investor who started with good intent drifts away not because markets crashed, but because there is no supported path through a temporary setback.

## Existing user workarounds
- Ask friends / family / WhatsApp groups whether to stop (mixed, often wrong advice)
- Stop the SIP entirely and intend to "restart later" (most never do)
- Reduce by manually cancelling and starting a new smaller SIP (friction, lost momentum)
- Simply stop checking and let the SIP lapse silently

## Why solving this creates meaningful user value
The investor keeps the compounding clock running. A temporary pause preserved is far better than a permanent stop, because restarting later means losing months of market time and often never resuming. The user avoids the regret of "I should have stayed invested."

## Why it makes business sense
12-month continuation is the north-star metric. Each percentage point of continuation raises customer lifetime value and improves investor outcomes — the core of Groww's value proposition. Intervening at months 3–6 captures users before they fully lapse, when re-activation is cheapest.

## NOT the problem
"Investors stop their SIPs when markets fall." — Our research shows continuation breaks down even when markets are flat, because the real issue is the lack of a graceful pause option, missing progress context, and silent debit failure — not market direction alone.
